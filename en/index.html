EXPEDITION ARCHITECTURE · 4 PILLARS

An indivisible 4-day intellectual immersion

01

Indivisible 4-Day Product

Participation covers all 4 days as one continuous philosophical narrative from opening dinner to closing synthesis—no partial weekend split.

02

Private Conference Hall & Recommended Hotel

We rent a private conference room in the city center for morning and evening lectures. Accommodation is booked independently by each participant — we share our recommended seminar hotel in advance so anyone who wishes can stay under the same roof.

03

Group of 4–10 & Museum Letters

Groups range from 4 to 10 participants, with formal museum excursion notifications sent ahead to ensure seamless gallery access.

04

Pre-Registration Without Payment

No payment is charged on this website. Once a minimum group of 4 forms, we confirm the program fee, seminar room schedule, and our hotel recommendation.

* Note: If only 1–2 participants sign up in a city instead of 4–10, Sergii’s scheduled research trip still takes place and we can offer a compact 3-hour private museum walk and coffee discussion.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Key Details About the Updated Format

Why is there no 2-day weekend-only option?

The expedition is designed as one indivisible philosophical narrative. Skipping Day 1 arrival context and Day 2 morning foundations breaks the arc of the finale.

How do hotel accommodation and meals work?

Participation covers strictly the 4-day intellectual program: lectures, our rented central conference hall, printed workbooks, and registered museum visits. Travel, hotel accommodation, and meals are booked and paid for directly by each participant — we share a recommended central hotel where our seminar hall is located so anyone who wishes can stay under the same roof.

What happens after submitting pre-registration?

Your details go to Sergii Grygoryshyn (@Grygoryshyn). Once 4+ guests are confirmed for a city, we confirm exact dates and program pricing and share our recommended hotel for your direct booking.

Do I need a background in philosophy or art history?

Not at all. Try the interactive “1840 ↔ 1843” section above for a live taste of how Sergii connects visual art, history, and philosophy.
